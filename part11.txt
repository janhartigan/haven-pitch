<p class="lead">Our Cold Salvage crew was all human, which wastes the best thing space offers. Here are five species built around body plans, not foreheads, each shown in and out of its suit. The suit is where the design lives: it has to solve what the body cannot survive.</p></div>

<div class="riskgrid"><ul class="risks">
<li><div><b>Readability at token scale</b><span>True scale runs from 60 centimetres to 3 metres, which no hex can hold. We would draw every hero to fit one hex, put the Durr on an oversized base and raise the Tikkri on a stand. Each species also needs one colour of its own, because the Murmur and Sarn blur together when small.</span></div><span class="pill hi">High</span></li>
<li><div><b>Art cost per species</b><span>Every species needs two looks, suited for scenarios and unsuited for the ship, plus its own rig. Humans share one rig. Our estimate, not a measurement: Durr, Tikkri and Sarn are medium, while the Thren, with liquid in a glass dome, and the Murmur, with a swarm, are high.</span></div><span class="pill hi">High</span></li>
<li><div><b>How many the first campaign needs</b><span>Gloomhaven opens with six starting classes, each from a different people. Our starter crew is four. Every species also needs a voice, a culture and a place on the ship, and writing is the cost people forget.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li>
<li><div><b>The Sarn undercuts a pillar</b><span>A hero that ignores vacuum weakens our first pillar, that the environment is the enemy. It is also the closest to Gloomhaven’s stone and crystal peoples, so it is the most likely to read as borrowed.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li>
</ul>
<div class="rec"><p class="eyebrow" style="margin:0">Our recommendation</p><p>Launch with four: humans, the Durr, the Tikkri and the Murmur, as the four existing starters (Cutter, Breacher, Runner and Tender). Hold the Thren and the Sarn as the first two unlock classes, the Thren because liquid is the hardest render and the Sarn because it needs the vacuum rules tuned around it first.</p><p>If the art budget only allows three, cut the Murmur first and bring it back as the first expansion. Water, wet surfaces and volumetric fog are the hardest of the three themes to render, and this is our estimate, not a measured cost.</span></div><span class="pill hi">High</span></li><li><div><b>Dynamic terrain complexity</b><span>Scheduled flooding is easy to explain and hard to balance. Scenarios would need to be designed around the schedule from the start.</span></div><span class="pill hi">High</span></li><li><div><b>Horror fatigue</b><span>Folk horror works in small doses. A long campaign needs warm hub scenes or the mood becomes a drone.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li><li><div><b>Echoes of familiar monsters</b><span>Drowned dead can read as generic undead. The Choir's pull and song are meant to avoid that, but the risk is real.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li></ul><div class="oq"><h4>Open questions</h4><ol><li>Is the core willing to take a scheduled terrain feature, or must tide be written as scenario text?</li><li>How do we communicate the next tide state without cluttering the board?</li><li>Can the art pipeline hold a consistent water look at an acceptable cost?</li></ol></div></div></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="compare"><div class="wrap"><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">Side by side</p><h2>The three themes compared</h2><p class="lead" style="margin-top:16px;font-size:18px">Every rating is our judgement. The Salt Road is the most distinctive theme and the safest check that the core is theme agnostic.</p>
<ul class="reqs" style="margin-top:40px"><li><b>Why not the Salt Road as the lead <span class="pill md" style="display:inline-flex;margin-left:8px">Runner up</span></b><span>Only because of cost and risk, not quality. Cloth and ornament are slower to produce well, and cultural sourcing needs review we have not arranged.</span></li><li><b>Why not Tidewatch as the lead <span class="pill md" style="display:inline-flex;margin-left:8px">Expansion candidate</span></b><span>It has the best atmosphere and the largest change to the rules core, plus the highest art cost. We would revisit it for an expansion.</span></li></ul></div>
<ol class="recsteps pcpad"><li><div><b>Run the swap test on five space scenario premises</b><span>On paper, before any art or code. Any premise that survives a fantasy swap is cut.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Build one scenario in Cold Salvage</b><span>A single compartment with air as the clock, using only the cards and rules the core has today.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Skin the same scenario data as the Salt Road</b><span>Same rules and data, new names and terrain. This tests the design check from the notes.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Compare and decide</b><span>If the space version only works because of laser guns, or cannot say anything that fantasy cannot, we switch the lead.</span></div></li></ol></div></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="questions"><div class="wrap"><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">Questions for you</p><h2>Five questions that push back on the lean toward space</h2></div><ol class="qs"><li>If you could only keep one of the three, and space were off the table, which would you pick? If the answer is not space, why do you lean toward it?</li><li>Space asks for hazard hexes, forced movement and an air clock. Are you willing to accept theme led rules in the core, or must every theme be a pure skin? If it is a pure skin, we should be honest that Cold Salvage is fantasy with lasers.</li><li>Do you want guns to be strong? Players of space games expect them. Making them weak keeps the hex tactics interesting and may disappoint people who came for blasters.</li><li>Do you want dread, awe or warmth in the first ten minutes of play? Each theme gives a different answer, and the tone should drive the choice more than the mechanics.</li><li>How much risk are you willing to carry on art and review? Tidewatch costs the most to render and the Salt Road needs outside cultural review. Space is the cheapest. Are you choosing space because you want it, or because it is cheap?</li></ol></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="sources"><div class="wrap"><div class="grid2" style="gap:72px"><div><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">How to read the art</p><h2>Concept art, not final art</h2></div><p style="font-size:17px">Every image in this readout is AI generated concept art, used as a mood board to set direction.
