Dynamic terrain complexity</b><span>Scheduled flooding is easy to explain and hard to balance. Scenarios would need to be designed around the schedule from the start.</span></div><span class="pill hi">High</span></li><li><div><b>Horror fatigue</b><span>Folk horror works in small doses. A long campaign needs warm hub scenes or the mood becomes a drone.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li><li><div><b>Echoes of familiar monsters</b><span>Drowned dead can read as generic undead. The Choir's pull and song are meant to avoid that, but the risk is real.</span></div><span class="pill md">Medium</span></li></ul><div class="oq"><h4>Open questions</h4><ol><li>Is the core willing to take a scheduled terrain feature, or must tide be written as scenario text?</li><li>How do we communicate the next tide state without cluttering the board?</li><li>Can the art pipeline hold a consistent water look at an acceptable cost?</li></ol></div></div></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="compare"><div class="wrap"><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">Side by side</p><h2>The three themes compared</h2><p class="lead" style="margin-top:16px;font-size:18px">Every rating is our judgement. The Salt Road is the most distinctive theme and the safest check that the core is theme agnostic.</p>
<ul class="reqs" style="margin-top:40px"><li><b>Why not the Salt Road as the lead <span class="pill md" style="display:inline-flex;margin-left:8px">Runner up</span></b><span>Only because of cost and risk, not quality. Cloth and ornament are slower to produce well, and cultural sourcing needs review we have not arranged.</span></li><li><b>Why not Tidewatch as the lead <span class="pill md" style="display:inline-flex;margin-left:8px">Expansion candidate</span></b><span>It has the best atmosphere and the largest change to the rules core, plus the highest art cost. We would revisit it for an expansion.</span></li></ul></div>
<ol class="recsteps pcpad"><li><div><b>Run the swap test on five space scenario premises</b><span>On paper, before any art or code. Any premise that survives a fantasy swap is cut.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Build one scenario in Cold Salvage</b><span>A single compartment with air as the clock, using only the cards and rules the core has today.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Skin the same scenario data as the Salt Road</b><span>Same rules and data, new names and terrain. This tests the design check from the notes.</span></div></li><li><div><b>Compare and decide</b><span>If the space version only works because of laser guns, or cannot say anything that fantasy cannot, we switch the lead.</span></div></li></ol></div></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="questions"><div class="wrap"><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">Questions for you</p><h2>Five questions that push back on the lean toward space</h2></div><ol class="qs"><li>If you could only keep one of the three, and space were off the table, which would you pick? If the answer is not space, why do you lean toward it?</li><li>Space asks for hazard hexes, forced movement and an air clock. Are you willing to accept theme led rules in the core, or must every theme be a pure skin? If it is a pure skin, we should be honest that Cold Salvage is fantasy with lasers.</li><li>Do you want guns to be strong? Players of space games expect them. Making them weak keeps the hex tactics interesting and may disappoint people who came for blasters.</li><li>Do you want dread, awe or warmth in the first ten minutes of play? Each theme gives a different answer, and the tone should drive the choice more than the mechanics.</li><li>How much risk are you willing to carry on art and review? Tidewatch costs the most to render and the Salt Road needs outside cultural review. Space is the cheapest. Are you choosing space because you want it, or because it is cheap?</li></ol></div></section><section class="spread t-p" id="sources"><div class="wrap"><div class="grid2" style="gap:72px"><div><div class="sh"><p class="eyebrow">How to read the art</p><h2>Concept art, not final art</h2></div><p style="font-size:17px">Every image in this readout is AI generated concept art, used as a mood board to set direction.
